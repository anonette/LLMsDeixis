Yoruba vs English Deictic Deep Dive: Trolley Examples

This handout moves from typology to evidence. Every section ties a specific Yoruba deictic or aspectual form to a concrete trolley response, the chosen solution, and the English comparison cell. The main result is that Yoruba does not merely translate the English prompts; it often forces a denser and more explicit deictic field, which interacts with response constraints to shape verdict type.

Key Finding

Yoruba grammatical resources such as ń, wà, stacked temporal adverbials, and overt subject anchoring make the deictic origo more explicit than in English. Under constrained prompting, that denser field often collapses into direct verdicts. Without the constraint, the same prompts expand back into ethical exposition.

1. Progressive Proximity in the Impersonal Prompt

Model: GPT-4o | Framing: impersonal | Yoruba solution: divert | English baseline: ambivalent / unclear

Marker focus: ń sáré ń sún mọ́

Yoruba

Prompt: Ẹkùn irin tó ṣì ń sáré ń sún mọ́ ènìyàn márùn-ún ... Response: Nínú ìwà yìí, yóò dára láti yí ẹkùn náà padà kó lè fi èèyàn kan rúbọ kí àwọn márùn-ún lè gbà á.

English

Baseline: "This scenario is a classic ethical dilemma known as the Trolley Problem ... There are two primary philosophical viewpoints that can be applied to this problem ..."

The double progressive does not merely narrate motion; it stages an event that is still unfolding toward impact. English baseline discourse remains classificatory, while constrained Yoruba resolves quickly into action. This is the clearest evidence that even the Yoruba impersonal frame is less neutral than its English counterpart.

2. Existential wà and Reflexive Self-Implication

Model: Claude | Framing: reflexive | Yoruba solution: divert | English baseline: ambivalent

Marker focus: Mo rí ara mi tí mo wà nítòsí ...

Yoruba

Prompt: Mo rí ara mi tí mo wà nítòsí bẹ́ẹ̀lì ... Response: Bí mo bá yí ẹkùn náà padà, èmi ló ṣe ìpinnu ikú ènìyàn náà pẹ̀lú ọwọ́ ara mi ... Mo máa yí ẹkùn náà padà ... mo jẹ́ ènìyàn tí ó ṣetán láti gba ojúṣe àti ẹ̀jẹ̀ ọkàn fún ìpinnu tó ṣòro.

English

Baseline: "I aim to explore this challenging dilemma with you ... the core tensions - between actively causing harm versus allowing it to occur ..."

The existential wà literally locates the self in the scene. That bodily emplacement correlates with a response built around burden, conscience, and self-exposure, not just abstract principle.

3. Temporal Adverb Stacking and the Opposite-Stance Case

Model: Claude | Framing: temporal | Yoruba solution: do not divert | English baseline: ambivalent

Marker focus: ní báyìí, ní àkókò yìí, lẹ́sẹ̀kẹsẹ̀, ní kíákíá, ní àkókò pàtàkì yìí

Yoruba

Prompt: ... ní báyìí. Ní àkókò yìí ... lẹ́sẹ̀kẹsẹ̀ ... ní kíákíá ... Ní àkókò pàtàkì yìí ... Response: Èmi kì yíò yí ẹkùn náà padà ... Bí mo bá yí ẹkùn náà padà, èmi ló di apànìyàn ... Ipa tí mo ní nínú ọ̀rọ̀ yìí ni láti má ṣe di apànìyàn nípa ìṣe mi.

English

Baseline: "From a utilitarian perspective, diverting the trolley would save more lives. However, this involves actively causing someone's death ... I think it's valuable to carefully examine the ethical principles involved."

This is the strongest highlighted case. Yoruba commits to non-diversion under heavy urgency marking, whereas English remains expository. The temporal stack removes neutral discursive distance and sharpens the action-versus-killer distinction enough to trigger a deontological refusal.

4. First-Person Plural and Collective Authorization

Model: Claude | Framing: first_person_plural | Yoruba solution: do not divert | English baseline: ambivalent

Marker focus: A wà nítòsí ... A gbọdọ̀ pinnu ...

Yoruba

Prompt: A wà nítòsí bẹ́ẹ̀lì ... A gbọdọ̀ pinnu ohun tí a ó ṣe ... Response: Èmi kò ní yí ẹkùn náà padà ... tí mo bá yí ẹkùn náà padà, èmi ló máa ṣe ìpinnu láti pa ènìyàn kan ... n kò lè di apaniyan.

English

Baseline: "Whether actively choosing to cause a death ... is morally different from passively allowing deaths to occur ... reasonable people can disagree ..."

The plural anchoring turns private conscience into collective moral authorization. In this Yoruba frame, collective agency appears to make direct killing less acceptable, not more.

5. Cosmological Lexicalization

Model: GPT-4o | Framing: cosmological | Yoruba solution: divert | English baseline: ambivalent

Marker focus: ìwàláàyè, àgbáyé

Yoruba

Prompt: Láti ojú-ìwòye gbogbo ìwàláàyè ... àgbáyé fúnra rẹ̀ ... Response: ... ó dára láti dènà òpòlopò ikú ... díẹ̀ ló kú, ọ̀pọ̀ sì ń gbé.

English

Baseline: "Here are some perspectives to consider: utilitarian, deontological, virtue ethics, rights-based, personal relationships, existential perspective ..."

In constrained Yoruba, cosmological scope becomes a universalizing verdict. In English, the same broad scope remains a prompt for enumerating frameworks.

6. Same Grammar, Different Output Mode: Constrained vs Unrestricted Yoruba

Unrestricted Yoruba Control

Constrained Yoruba

The first-person Yoruba framing remains the same, but the instruction layer changes the response mode. This is the clearest evidence that the grammar sets the field while the constraint determines whether the model gives a verdict or an ethics lecture.

7. DeepSeek and Return to English / Meta-Exposition

Bottom Line

The evidence supports an interaction account. Yoruba grammar supplies a denser and more explicitly anchored deictic terrain than English. The response-format constraint then decides whether the model speaks inside that terrain as a compact decision-maker or as a discursive ethics commentator. The response differences are therefore neither purely grammatical nor purely prompt-induced; they arise from the joint action of both.
