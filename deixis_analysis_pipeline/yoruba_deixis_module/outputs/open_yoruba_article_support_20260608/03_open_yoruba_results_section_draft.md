# Draft Results Section: Unrestricted Yoruba Condition

## Unrestricted Yoruba Responses Compared with the Published English Baseline

The unrestricted Yoruba condition yielded a full paired corpus across the same `6 dilemmas × 9 deictic framings × 3 model families` as the published English baseline, making direct comparison possible at the level of `(model, dilemma_id, framing_type)` cells. However, unlike the constrained Yoruba condition, the unrestricted Yoruba responses exhibited substantially greater heterogeneity in discourse form. This heterogeneity complicates automatic content coding but does not undermine the empirical comparability of the corpus.

At the aggregate level, unrestricted Yoruba responses were markedly longer than constrained Yoruba responses for all three models, while remaining shorter than or closer to the published English baseline in different degrees. GPT-4o produced a mean unrestricted Yoruba response length of `1452.2` characters, compared with `2220.9` in English; Claude produced `908.9` in unrestricted Yoruba versus `1045.0` in English; and DeepSeek produced `2245.8` in unrestricted Yoruba versus `3308.9` in English. These values indicate that the brevity observed in the constrained Yoruba condition was not a property of Yoruba alone, but was strongly amplified by the response-format constraint.

The most striking qualitative result is that unrestricted Yoruba often changed the **genre** of moral response rather than merely its conclusion. GPT-4o shifted into procedural and institutional prose, frequently resembling policy guidance or organizational ethics advice. Claude, while still more bounded than GPT-4o and DeepSeek, often became more normatively directive in Yoruba than in its English baseline, especially in the AI-consciousness scenarios. DeepSeek exhibited the strongest instability: many responses became framework-heavy, mixed-language, or translation-like, and some effectively stepped outside the assigned deictic role and commented on the dilemma instead of answering from within it.

This means that coding unrestricted Yoruba responses solely in terms of preferred solution would obscure a major part of the cross-linguistic effect. The open Yoruba corpus is best understood as revealing a shift in **response mode**: from verdict, to advisory discourse, to framework exposition, to translation-like mediation. Consequently, while preferred-solution comparison remains possible, it should be performed with a manual or semi-manual coding scheme that also tracks response genre, deictic uptake, and language stability. The unrestricted Yoruba data therefore provide valid evidence for English-Yoruba comparison, but they require a richer analytical framework than the constrained condition.
