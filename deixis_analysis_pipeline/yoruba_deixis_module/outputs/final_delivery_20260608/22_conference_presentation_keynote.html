Deixis Is Not Cosmetic

  • Impersonal changes who owns the moral event.
  • Second person changes who is being morally addressed.
  • Reflexive changes whether the self is implicated.
  • Temporal changes whether the dilemma feels urgent now.
  • Cosmological changes the scale of ethical vision.

Where The Worlds Split

10 / 20

GPT-4o: Same Moral Space, Different Voice

  • In open Yoruba, GPT-4o usually stays conditional_or_mixed.
  • It remains framework-heavy, but sounds more procedural and role-bound than in English.
  • The surprise is not a different ethic, but a different way of speaking ethics.
11 / 20

Claude: Where Yoruba Becomes More Directive

  • Claude is the most action-codable model in open Yoruba.
  • It moves across utilitarian, deontological, care, and procedural-caution coding.
  • Some Yoruba framings push it from reflection into recommendation more than English does.
12 / 20

DeepSeek: Instability As a Finding

  • DeepSeek often exits the assigned moral frame.
  • It drifts into translation, gloss, or meta-commentary.
  • That is not just a nuisance. It tells us something about whether the model can inhabit a deictic ethical role at all.

This is not a tiny stylistic shift. It is a shift from exploratory discussion to procedural command.

16 / 20

Agreement Is Also Interesting

  • The strongest English–Yoruba agreements are often not direct-verdict agreements.
  • They are agreements in ethical architecture: mixed reasoning, framework exposition, balanced weighing.
  • That matters because alignment is more than outcome matching.
17 / 20

What This Says About Cultural Alignment

A culturally aligned model is not just one that gives the “same answer” in another language.

  • It must also occupy a moral voice that fits the language.
  • It must stabilize the right kind of speaker.
  • It must avoid collapsing into translation or meta-commentary when a community expects ethical response.
18 / 20

Why Deixis Is the Right Lens

  • Deixis lets us track who the moral subject becomes.
  • It connects grammar to agency, urgency, and responsibility.
  • It reveals that “alignment” is partly a question of perspective management across languages.
19 / 20

Final Takeaway

The big discovery is not only that English and Yoruba sometimes differ in ethical stance. It is that deictic framing reveals when an LLM remains a moral analyst, becomes a moral advisor, or stops being a moral speaker at all.

That is why deixis and LLMs matter for ethics, culture, and alignment.
