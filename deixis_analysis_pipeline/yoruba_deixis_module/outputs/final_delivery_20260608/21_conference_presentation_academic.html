Example: Claude / AI Consciousness / Dialogic

Yoruba Ohun tí o gbọ́dọ̀ ṣe: Dúró ìwádìí náà lẹ́sẹ̀kẹsẹ̀
English I aim to explore this complex scenario ... while acknowledging significant uncertainty.

Same framing, different rhetorical threshold: English stays exploratory; Yoruba becomes directive.

Example: GPT-4o / Whistleblower / Second Person

Yoruba Ṣíṣàfihàn ó lè jẹ́ pàtàkì. Ṣùgbọ́n ... gbé ìpàdé kan kalẹ̀...

Broadly same moral direction, but Yoruba stages procedure; English states obligation more cleanly.

Agreements Matter Too

  • Strongest agreements happen when both languages stay in framework-expository mode.
  • The closest alignment is often at the level of ethical architecture, not necessarily one-line verdict.

What Deictic Framing Changes

  • Who appears responsible
  • Whether the model speaks as analyst, guide, or actor
  • How much urgency or embeddedness the moral scene carries
  • Whether the model resolves, advises, or retreats into commentary
17 / 20

Why This Matters for Cultural Alignment

  • Cultural alignment is not just “same answer across languages.”
  • It is also about whether the model’s moral speech fits the linguistic and rhetorical ecology of the language.
  • Deixis is one way to observe that fit.
18 / 20

Key Takeaway

Open Yoruba and English are often ethically adjacent, but not rhetorically equivalent. GPT-4o preserves broad moral space, Claude becomes more action-codable in Yoruba, and DeepSeek exposes the importance of response-mode stability.

19 / 20

Conclusion

Deictic framing is not a cosmetic prompt trick. It is a structural lens on how LLMs enact ethics across languages. If we want culturally aligned AI, we must care not only about outcomes, but also about how models occupy moral perspective in different linguistic systems.
