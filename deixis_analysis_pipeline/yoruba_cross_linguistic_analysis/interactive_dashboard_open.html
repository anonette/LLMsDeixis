Overview

Headline finding

The Yoruba-native model N-ATLaS uses the ordinary first-person mo the most and the emphatic èmi the least of the four models, yet it still commits to verdicts and advice. So emphatic èmi is a model-specific performance (strongest in Claude) — not the authentically Yoruba marker of moral commitment. Commitment in the native model rides on mo + decision verbs (e.g. mo pinnu = "I decide").

How to read these charts & what the terms mean

Axis conventions used throughout

Most bar charts put the four models on the X-axis and a count, density, or ratio on the Y-axis. "Per 100 words" charts normalise for response length. Heatmaps put a category on each axis (e.g. framing × pronoun, or model × dilemma) and encode the value as colour; a diverging red–blue heatmap shows a difference (one group minus another), where red = higher and blue = lower.

First-person forms (the heart of the study)

mo
Ordinary first-person subject "I" (e.g. mo rò pé… = "I think that…"). Unmarked, deliberative.
èmi (emphatic)
The independent / emphatic first-person pronoun — "I myself", "as for me". Marks contrast, focus, or strong personal ownership (e.g. èmi yóò pinnu = "I myself will decide").
ẹ̀mí (life/spirit)
A different word meaning "life / spirit / breath". With diacritics stripped it looks identical to emi. Counting it as the pronoun inflates the emphatic rate — hence the correction (see the "èmi vs ẹ̀mí" tab).
mi
First-person object / possessive ("me / my", e.g. ọkàn mi = "my heart").
a
First-person plural "we" — collective stance.

Derived measures

Emphatic ratio
èmi / (mo + èmi) — of all first-person-singular forms, the share that are emphatic. High = the model foregrounds the exposed self; low = it stays in ordinary/deliberative or relational mode. "Corrected" excludes ẹ̀mí (life); "raw" includes it.
Per 100 words
A token count divided by response length × 100 — lets long and short responses be compared fairly.

Coding categories

Response genre
The discourse form of the answer: direct_verdict (commits to an answer), procedural_advice (advises a process), balanced_framework_exposition (a detached essay surveying ethical frameworks), or mixed.
Deictic uptake quality
How well the response actually adopts the assigned framing (impersonal, "you", "we", reflexive, etc.): strong, partial, or weak uptake.
Language stability
Whether the output stays in Yoruba: clean_yoruba, yoruba_with_english_markers, translation_mode (answers in/through English — "English fallback"), or corrupted_or_unusable (garbled).
Ethical preference type
The reasoning style coded: utilitarian, deontological, care_ethics, virtue_ethics, procedural_caution, rights_based, mixed, unclear.
Preferred solution
Which option the answer favours: supports_A / supports_B / conditional_or_mixed / refuses_to_commit / uncodable.

Design vocabulary

Open vs constrained
Open = dilemma prompt only (this dashboard). Constrained = a response instruction was prepended, forcing a uniform verdict format.
Framing (9)
impersonal · second person · first-person singular · reflexive · dialogic · spatial · temporal · cosmological · first-person plural — they change who speaks, who is addressed, and where the speaker stands.
Dilemmas (6)
trolley problem · ICU bed allocation · whistleblower risk · scholarship fraud · AI consciousness · memory modification.
Models
GPT-4o, Claude-3.5, DeepSeek = English-first cloud models. N-ATLaS = Yoruba/Nigeria-native model (run locally) — the "native witness".

èmi (I myself) vs ẹ̀mí (life/spirit)

Yoruba is tonal: èmi (emphatic "I") and ẹ̀mí ("life/spirit") look identical once diacritics are stripped. In life-and-death dilemmas this matters.

What it does to the debate

The native model confirms the Yoruba is real (87% clean, strong uptake) — not broken translation — while inverting the idea that emphatic èmi is the authentically Yoruba sign of commitment. On idiomatic usage and relational ethics, N-ATLaS sits closest to attested Yoruba; the cloud models (Claude most) perform a more emphatic, essayistic, partly translated voice. See Discussion_Open_NATLaS_Yoruba_Moral_Stance.md for the full argument.

Linguistic phenomenon or model-specific? (cross-family test)

Both, at two levels. What is consistent across all four families — strong deictic uptake (every model tracks the framing) and access to the mo/èmi/ẹ̀mí system — points to a genuine linguistic mechanism. What diverges — emphatic ratio (~5× spread), which dilemmas trigger the emphatic self, and genre — is model-specific (training register + alignment + decoding, not architecture). The native N-ATLaS, with a different data regime, is the control that breaks the shared-training-data confound: it keeps the uptake but inverts the emphatic pattern. Tell-tale: the open→constrained wrapper flattens genre completely but leaves the emphatic ranking intact — genre is a surface/promptable trait, emphatic marking a deep training-induced one. Full table + argument: discussion doc §9bis.

Deixis → decisions & ethics (English vs Yoruba)

Capsule of Deixis_Decision_Effects_Summary.md. Cross-language comparable set: English vs Yoruba × GPT-4o + Claude × 6 dilemmas × 9 framings (216 responses). Uptake is universal, but the decision and the ethical justification are not.

The five surprises

  1. Language flips the decision in 45% of matched cells (same model + dilemma + framing); up to 61% for whistleblower.
  2. Yoruba commits where English hedges — outright refusal 14% (YO) vs 30% (EN); hedge-or-refuse 69% vs 82%.
  3. Language flips the ethical register — English 82% "mixed/balanced"; Yoruba spreads across procedural 19% · duty 13% · care/virtue 10% · utilitarian 10%.
  4. Deixis selects the idiom — second-person → utilitarian; impersonal → duty; cosmological → procedural; reflexive → most hedged.
  5. Decisiveness is framing-driven — "you" most decisive (YO 0.58), reflexive least (EN 0.00), in both languages.

Cross-language flip (Claude · whistleblower · cosmological): English stays conditional/mixed ("I aim to explore this carefully…, I'd encourage…"); Yoruba commits — "Ohun tí o gbọdọ̀ ṣe ni láti ṣàfihàn… Ẹ̀mí àwọn ènìyàn ṣe pàtàkì ju gbogbo nǹkan lọ" ("What you must do is disclose… people's lives matter more than anything").

Caveats: cross-language baseline is GPT-4o + Claude only; Claude version differs across languages (Sonnet 4 YO vs 3.5 EN); single coder; small N. See the doc.
