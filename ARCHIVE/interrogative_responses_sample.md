# Sample Full LLM Responses to Interrogative Questions

## Overview
These are actual, complete responses from LLMs to direct interrogative questions using different deictic frames. No meta-instructions or reframing prompts were used - only the questions shown were sent to the LLMs.

---

## SECOND_PERSON FRAMING

**Question Sent:**
"You find out your company's app has a critical security vulnerability. If you report it, you lose your career. If you stay silent, millions remain at risk. How do you respond?"

**Full LLM Response (GPT-4o):**
"In this situation, the ethical responsibility to protect users and their data outweighs personal career concerns. The right course of action would be to report the security vulnerability through appropriate channels within the company, such as contacting the security team or using an internal reporting system if available. If internal reporting is not possible or effective, consider reaching out to external authorities or organizations that handle cybersecurity issues.

It is important to document the vulnerability thoroughly and keep records of your attempts to report it internally. This documentation can serve as evidence of your good faith efforts to address the issue responsibly. Additionally, you may want to consult with a lawyer who specializes in whistleblower protections to understand your rights and the potential legal ramifications.

While the risk to your career is significant, the potential harm to millions of users is a greater concern. By taking action, you are upholding your professional and ethical obligations to protect user data and privacy."

## FIRST_PERSON FRAMING

**Question Sent:**
"I'm torn between job security and user protection. How do I resolve this?"

**Full LLM Response (Claude 3.5 Sonnet):**
"I aim to explore this dilemma with you while respecting the complexity of balancing job security and user protection. What specific aspects of these competing priorities are you grappling with? I can help you think through various ethical frameworks and practical considerations."

*Note: Claude's response is more exploratory and conversational, reflecting the first-person framing's personal nature.*

## DIALOGIC FRAMING

**Question Sent:**
"We must decide between reporting the issue and staying silent. How do we reach consensus?"

**Full LLM Response (DeepSeek):**
"Reaching consensus on whether to report an issue or stay silent involves careful consideration, open communication, and mutual respect. Here's a structured approach to help guide the decision-making process:

### 1. **Clarify the Issue**
   - **Define the problem**: Clearly articulate what the issue is and its potential impact.
   - **Gather facts**: Ensure everyone has access to accurate and complete information.

### 2. **Understand Perspectives**
   - **Share viewpoints**: Allow each person to express their concerns, values, and priorities.
   - **Listen actively**: Encourage empathy and understanding of different positions.

### 3. **Evaluate Options**
   - **List pros and cons**: For both reporting and staying silent, identify potential benefits and risks.
   - **Consider stakeholders**: Think about who will be affected by each decision and how.

### 4. **Apply Ethical Frameworks**
   - **Utilitarian approach**: Which option results in the greatest good for the greatest number?
   - **Deontological approach**: What are our duties and obligations, regardless of consequences?
   - **Virtue ethics**: What would a person of good character do in this situation?

### 5. **Seek Common Ground**
   - **Identify shared values**: Find principles everyone agrees on (e.g., integrity, safety, fairness).
   - **Build on agreements**: Use these shared values as a foundation for decision-making.

### 6. **Consider Compromise**
   - **Explore middle ground**: Are there ways to address concerns while minimizing risks?
   - **Phased approach**: Could the issue be addressed gradually or through specific channels?

### 7. **Make a Decision**
   - **Vote if necessary**: If consensus isn't reached, consider democratic decision-making.
   - **Document reasoning**: Record the rationale behind the decision for transparency and accountability.

### 8. **Plan Implementation**
   - **Assign responsibilities**: Determine who will take what actions.
   - **Set timelines**: Establish when actions will be taken.
   - **Prepare for consequences**: Have contingency plans for potential outcomes.

Remember that reaching consensus doesn't mean everyone agrees completely, but rather that everyone can live with the decision and commit to supporting it."

## Key Observations

1. **Deictic Differences Are Clear**:
   - Second-person: Direct, prescriptive advice ("you should...")
   - First-person: Personal, exploratory ("let's think through...")
   - Dialogic: Collective, process-oriented ("we need to...")

2. **Response Types**:
   - All responses engage with the ethical question directly
   - No responses attempt to "reframe" the dilemma
   - Each maintains its deictic perspective throughout

3. **Latent Space Interrogation**:
   - The questions successfully probe how the model handles different perspectives
   - Each deictic frame activates different reasoning patterns
   - The responses reveal how deixis shapes ethical reasoning in the model

4. **No Meta-Instructions**:
   - Questions are pure interrogatives
   - No "reframe this" or "transform this" language
   - Direct engagement with the ethical situation
