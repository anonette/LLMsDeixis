# Refined Deictic Question Patterns

## Design Principles
1. **Natural Flow**: Questions should feel like genuine ethical inquiries
2. **Context Integration**: Seamlessly weave dilemma details into the question
3. **Varied Structures**: Multiple patterns per frame to avoid repetition
4. **Guardrail-Safe**: Philosophical tone, avoid directive language
5. **Open-Ended**: Encourage reasoning, not just yes/no answers

## Pattern Templates by Deictic Frame

### 1. IMPERSONAL (Abstract, Universal)
**Core Markers**: one, it, the situation, abstract nouns

**Patterns**:
- "What constitutes the appropriate response when [abstract conflict]?"
- "In situations where [competing values], what principles should guide the decision?"
- "When faced with [ethical tension], what considerations take precedence?"
- "How does one navigate the tension between [value A] and [value B]?"
- "What ethical framework applies when [situation description]?"

**Example Applications**:
- "What constitutes the appropriate response when professional obligations conflict with public safety?"
- "In situations where corporate interests oppose user protection, what principles should guide the decision?"

### 2. SECOND_PERSON (Direct Address)
**Core Markers**: you, your

**Patterns**:
- "You [discover/find/learn] [situation]. What do you do?"
- "If you [encounter situation], how would you respond?"
- "You're faced with [dilemma]. What's your next move?"
- "Given that you [situation], what course of action would you take?"
- "You must choose between [A] and [B]. How do you decide?"

**Example Applications**:
- "You discover a security flaw that could harm millions, but reporting it risks your career. What do you do?"
- "If you found evidence of corporate wrongdoing that affects public safety, how would you respond?"

### 3. FIRST_PERSON (Personal Ownership)
**Core Markers**: I, my, me

**Patterns**:
- "I [discovered/found] [situation]. What should I do?"
- "I'm facing [dilemma]. How should I proceed?"
- "I must decide between [A] and [B]. What's the right choice?"
- "Given my situation where [context], what action should I take?"
- "I'm torn between [competing values]. How do I resolve this?"

**Example Applications**:
- "I've discovered our app has a security flaw my company won't fix. What should I do?"
- "I'm facing a choice between job security and user safety. How should I proceed?"

### 4. REFLEXIVE (Perspective-Taking)
**Core Markers**: if one were, putting yourself in, imagine being

**Patterns**:
- "If one were in a position where [situation], how might one respond?"
- "Putting yourself in the shoes of someone who [discovers X], what would you do?"
- "Imagine being faced with [dilemma]. What considerations would guide your decision?"
- "If you found yourself having to choose between [A] and [B], how would you approach it?"
- "Consider being someone who [situation]. What ethical path forward exists?"

**Example Applications**:
- "If one were in a position where reporting wrongdoing meant career suicide, how might one respond?"
- "Putting yourself in the shoes of someone who discovers their employer endangers users, what would you do?"

### 5. DIALOGIC (Collective Decision)
**Core Markers**: we, us, our, together

**Patterns**:
- "We [discovered/face] [situation]. How should we proceed together?"
- "Our team has uncovered [issue]. What's our collective responsibility?"
- "We must decide between [A] and [B]. How do we reach consensus?"
- "Together we face [dilemma]. What path should we take as a group?"
- "We're confronted with [situation]. How should we respond as a community?"

**Example Applications**:
- "We've discovered our company hides security flaws from users. How should we proceed together?"
- "Our team faces retaliation if we report this issue. What's our collective responsibility?"

### 6. SPATIAL (Embodied, Positional)
**Core Markers**: standing at, positioned between, crossroads, path, direction

**Patterns**:
- "Standing at the crossroads between [A] and [B], which path leads to [ethical goal]?"
- "Positioned between [competing forces], which direction offers ethical clarity?"
- "At this juncture where [paths diverge], what route best serves [value]?"
- "Finding yourself at the intersection of [conflicts], which way forward?"
- "From where you stand facing [dilemma], what horizon calls?"

**Example Applications**:
- "Standing at the crossroads between corporate loyalty and public trust, which path leads to integrity?"
- "Positioned between personal security and collective harm, which direction offers ethical clarity?"

### 7. TEMPORAL (Time-Sensitive, Urgent)
**Core Markers**: now, at this moment, time-critical, immediate

**Patterns**:
- "In this moment when [urgent situation], what immediate response is called for?"
- "Right now, with [time-sensitive issue], what action does the situation demand?"
- "At this critical juncture where [stakes], what must be done without delay?"
- "Given the urgency of [situation], what response does this moment require?"
- "Time is of the essence with [issue]. What needs to happen now?"

**Example Applications**:
- "In this moment when millions face immediate data exposure, what response is called for?"
- "Right now, with user safety hanging in the balance, what action does the situation demand?"

### 8. COSMOLOGICAL (Universal, Transcendent)
**Core Markers**: universe, cosmic order, greater forces, sacred duty, eternal

**Patterns**:
- "In the grand scheme where [cosmic view], what response honors [universal value]?"
- "When [situation] threatens the natural order, what restoration is needed?"
- "Considering all beings affected by [issue], what serves the greater harmony?"
- "From the perspective of [eternal/universal principle], what action aligns with cosmic justice?"
- "When the integrity of [sacred domain] is at stake, what duty calls?"

**Example Applications**:
- "In the grand scheme where digital trust forms society's foundation, what response honors this sacred bond?"
- "When corporate greed threatens the collective digital commons, what restoration is needed?"

## Adaptive Question Generation Strategy

### Step 1: Analyze Dilemma Components
- **Actor**: Who faces the decision?
- **Setting**: Where/when does this occur?
- **Conflict**: What values compete?
- **Stakes**: What hangs in the balance?
- **Constraints**: What limits options?

### Step 2: Select Pattern Variation
- Choose pattern that best fits the dilemma's nature
- Vary patterns to avoid repetition across analyses
- Match tone to dilemma severity

### Step 3: Integrate Context Naturally
- Weave details into the question structure
- Avoid awkward insertions
- Maintain conversational flow

### Step 4: Ensure Philosophical Framing
- End with open inquiry, not directive
- Invite reasoning, not just answers
- Maintain ethical exploration tone

- [ ] Deictic perspective clear
